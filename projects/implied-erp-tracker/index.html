How the implied ERP is computed

The implied equity risk premium is the discount rate that equates today's index level to the present value of the cash flows equity holders expect, less the risk-free rate. Cash flow per index point (trailing dividends plus buybacks) grows at the expected earnings growth rate for five years, then at the risk-free rate in perpetuity. This is Damodaran's two-stage set-up.

Level = Σt=1..5 CFt / (1+Ke)t + CF5(1+Rf) / ((Ke−Rf)(1+Ke)5)  ·  ERP = Ke − Rf

Ke is solved by bisection to 1e-12. The index level enters only through the cash yield (cash flow ÷ level), so the daily close moves the ERP while the three assumptions stay fixed until you revise them. A falling ERP means prices are running ahead of the cash-flow inputs and the market is pricing less risk; a rising ERP means the opposite.

Indian payout ratios are low, so a dividend-only cash flow gives a lower implied ERP than the US series. For a payout-adjusted view, enter a sustainable cash flow per point, for example earnings per point × (1 − g ÷ ROE), in the cash flow field and keep the same basis every day.

Trend chips compare the latest close with the average of the last 20 readings (±0.5% band). The valuation chip places the latest ERP within this index's own history: bottom quartile reads as priced rich, top quartile as priced cheap. Both are rules of thumb, not a recommendation.
